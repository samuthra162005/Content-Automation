# Research Sources & Fact Verification

## 1. Authoritative Clinical Sources Evaluated

1. **American Association of Orthodontists (AAO) — Early Orthodontic Guidelines**
   - *Key Guidance:* Recommends an initial orthodontic screening no later than age 7. While most children do not receive full braces until ages 9–14, early evaluation identifies skeletal imbalances, crossbites, and severe crowding while jaws are growing.
   - *Application:* Anchors the age-suitability criteria in Section 1 and Section 2.

2. **American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry (AAPD) — Developing Dentition Standards**
   - *Key Guidance:* Highlights that pediatric compliance and plaque control differ significantly between fixed appliances (metal/ceramic) and removable aligners. Removable aligners require 20–22 hours of daily wear, making them best suited for disciplined older children.
   - *Application:* Guides the comparison matrix and compliance trade-offs.

3. **Indian Dental Association (IDA) — Child Dental Health Protocols**
   - *Key Guidance:* Emphasizes dietary discipline in Indian cuisine (avoiding hard snacks like murukku, sticky sweets like chikki) to prevent bracket debonding during active orthodontic treatment.
   - *Application:* Provides practical lifestyle advice relevant to Tamil Nadu families.

## 2. Verified Facts vs. Common Misconceptions

* **Misconception:** *"Braces can only begin once all permanent teeth erupt at age 12–13."*
  - **Verified Clinical Fact:** Phase 1 interceptive orthodontics frequently begins between ages 7 and 9 using partial braces, palatal expanders, or habit appliances to guide jaw growth before permanent teeth lock in crooked positions.
* **Misconception:** *"Clear aligners work identically for all children."*
  - **Verified Clinical Fact:** Aligners require strict 22-hour daily wear and are easily lost during school lunch. Fixed metal braces remain the gold standard for severe skeletal misalignments and younger children.
* **Misconception:** *"Ceramic braces are fragile and dangerous for active kids."*
  - **Verified Clinical Fact:** Modern polycrystalline ceramic brackets are highly durable but slightly more brittle than stainless steel, making them ideal for image-conscious preteens who do not participate in heavy contact sports.
